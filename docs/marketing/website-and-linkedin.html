Short answer

Don't make it LinkedIn-only. LinkedIn brings people in; the game lives at play.obliqueconsult.com.

LinkedIn doesn't let outside companies put a playable game inside it. A LinkedIn-only version would be polls and image posts: no scores, no streaks, no sign-ups, and no names in HubSpot. Keep the game on your own subdomain, link it from the website, and use LinkedIn (plus WhatsApp) to send people there. Every result someone shares sends new players back.

1. Someone sees itA LinkedIn post, a WhatsApp forward, or the Oblique website
2. Taps the linkA share card shows the score, e.g. "Sara scored 17/20. Can you beat it?"
3. PlaysThe same 20 questions, with the what and why after each
4. Signs inEmail and a 6-digit code. Becomes a HubSpot contact
5. Shares or booksChallenges a colleague, or books a free diagnostic on Calendly

Part 2

On LinkedIn

The game already makes the posts. When someone finishes a quiz, they can share a link that previews as their score card, or save the card as an image. Your job is to start the first wave and keep a weekly rhythm.

The card and the preview text are made by the game for every challenge link. The name and score on this card are test data.

Why this works

  • A score is a dare. "Can you beat 17/20?" gets replies that "we launched a game" doesn't.
  • It's fair. Everyone who taps the link gets the same questions in the same order, so scores compare.
  • Players post for you. Each person who shares is a new post in a new network, at no cost.
  • It works inside LinkedIn's app. Sign-in uses a 6-digit code, which players type in. They don't need to leave LinkedIn's browser to open an email link.
  • It shows expertise without selling. Every answer cites the law, which is what you'd want a prospect to see from Oblique.

Part 3

The first month, day by day

Personal profiles usually reach far more people than company pages, so most posts come from you and the team, with the company page resharing. A starting assumption for timing is weekday mornings, 8–10am UAE time. Check your own post stats after week 1 and adjust.

Before launch
  • Tue 10 Nov teaser

    "One thing on this invoice is wrong. Can you find it?" Use slides 1–3 of a Shift 1 invoice (npm run slides -- s1-03), with no link yet. Answer in the comments the next day.

  • Thu 12 Nov behind the scenes

    "Next Tuesday we're launching a free UAE tax game." Mention that every question cites the law and was reviewed by Oblique's tax team. Ask people to comment "in" to get the link on the day.

  • Sun 15 – Mon 16 Nov private messages

    Message 5 UAE tax voices and the Oblique team. Ask if they'll play on Tuesday and accept a tag. Seed 3–5 WhatsApp groups (alumni, finance, founders).

Launch week
  • Tue 17 Nov launch

    You post your real score card and tag the people who agreed. The team posts their own scores the same morning. Send the link to everyone who commented "in". Reply to every comment in the first hour. The Framer links and the two website boxes go live the same morning.

  • Thu 19 Nov poll

    First "hardest question" poll, from the question most players missed on days 1–2.

  • Fri 20 Nov results

    Answer and "why" to the poll, plus one line of real numbers, e.g. "X people played this week".

Weeks 2–4
  • Tue 24 Nov podcast challenge

    Beyond Decimals: host versus guest on the same quiz, filmed or recorded. Post both scores and challenge listeners. Also the first new weekly question batch.

  • Thu 26 Nov spot the error

    First full swipeable post: the 6 slides from npm run slides (hook → invoice in two halves → answer → why → link). The link can go straight to play.obliqueconsult.com/spot-the-error.

  • Every Tue and Thu rhythm

    Tuesday: hardest question poll. Thursday: spot the error. Weekdays: reshare the best player cards from the company page.

  • Reviewers Ashta, Pranay, Aidarous, Arslan

    One post each in the first four weeks (17 Nov to 15 Dec): "I helped review these questions. Here's the one that catches most people." Expert voices make it credible.

  • Tue 1 Dec first monthly leaderboard

    November's top players (17–30 Nov): a shout-out, not a prize, unless the legal check is done and you've decided to award November. Only players who chose to show their name get named.

  • Tue 5 Jan first prize winners

    If prizes start on 1 Dec: December's winners, named only with their permission.

About links in posts. Many people believe LinkedIn shows posts with outside links to fewer people. LinkedIn hasn't confirmed it. Test it in week 1: launch day with the link in the post (so the score card shows), then two posts with the image and the link in the first comment. Keep whichever reaches more people.

Part 5

Why not LinkedIn-only?

LinkedIn's own puzzle games are made by LinkedIn. Outside companies can't publish a playable game inside it. "LinkedIn-only" would therefore mean polls and image posts, which you're doing anyway as the way in.

LinkedIn onlyGame on play.obliqueconsult.com, promoted on LinkedIn
The actual game (20 questions, invoices, explanations)No. Polls allow one question and four short options.Yes. Built and tested.
Scores, streaks, leaderboards, certificatesNoYes
Knowing who playedOnly likes and commentsEmail, role and progress in HubSpot (marketing only with consent)
Free diagnostic bookingsIndirect, via messagesButton in the game → Calendly, name and email filled in
ReachLinkedIn onlyLinkedIn, plus WhatsApp, email, the website and search
Who controls the audienceLinkedIn's feed decides who sees itYou own the player list
Extra workLessAlready built; needs the accounts set up
Bottom line: do both, with different jobs. LinkedIn content (polls, invoices, score cards) gets attention. The game turns that attention into players, and players into contacts and diagnostic bookings.
Part 6

Numbers to watch

The game already records sign-ups, plays, which challenge link brought each player, and diagnostic clicks. Page views and drop-off need the analytics tool (PostHog), which isn't built yet.

Weekly scorecard

  • Players per day, and how many came from a challenge link
  • Sign-up rate: people who sign in ÷ people who start
  • Share rate: players who create a challenge ÷ players who finish
  • New players per shared link
  • Came back within 7 days: the best sign the game is good
  • Diagnostic clicks and Calendly bookings tagged "oblique-play"

How sharing adds up: an example

These are made-up starting numbers, not a forecast. Replace them with real ones after week 1.

Players from your launch posts200
Share rate (players who share)15%
New players per shared link2
Each player brings: 0.15 × 20.3 new players
Round 1: 200 × 0.360
Round 2: 60 × 0.318
Round 3: 18 × 0.35.4
All rounds: 200 × 0.3 ÷ (1 − 0.3)≈ 86 extra players

So in this example, sharing adds about 43% on top of what your posts bring in (86 ÷ 200 = 0.43). It never snowballs on its own, because each player brings fewer than one new player. That's why the Tuesday and Thursday posts matter: they restart the chain every week.
